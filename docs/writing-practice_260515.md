# Cult to Canon — Writing Practice

A working document on what to write, what contribution means, how to use Claude, and how to stop needing it.

## The thesis

The site's name is a thesis: canonization is a *process*, not a state, and the writer is one of its operators. Each essay is a small intervention in the apparatus by which cultural objects become legible. The site is not a journal of opinions; it is a workbench at which canon-formation work is done, one essay at a time.

Scaffolding: James English, *The Economy of Prestige* (Harvard, 2005); Pierre Bourdieu, "The Field of Cultural Production" (1993); Pascale Casanova, *The World Republic of Letters* (Harvard, 2004).

## Six native essay forms

1. **The Elevation** — sustained close reading arguing an object deserves more attention than it gets. (Fisher, *Ghosts of My Life*; Geoffrey O'Brien, *The Phantom Empire*.)
2. **The Genealogy** — how an object moved from cult to canon, and what that movement reveals about taste. (Carol Clover, *Men, Women, and Chain Saws*.)
3. **The Forecast** — naming what will matter. Riskiest form; dates badly, but the dating is itself useful.
4. **The Demolition** — arguing a canonical object's status is brittle or unearned. (Logan, Ricks, Wood.)
5. **The Lateral Comparison** — putting a cult and canonical object side-by-side to find the structural rhyme. (Cronenberg's *Crash* and Ballard; the *Backrooms* and Borges's "Library of Babel.")
6. **The Personal Canon** — the objects that formed you and what they have in common. The form where voice usually arrives.

Hybrid forms are productive. Most pieces should fit at least one.

## What to write about

### Horror (primary seed)

Definitionally canon-formation work — almost every canonical horror film was once disreputable. Veins worth working:

- Italian giallo (Argento, Bava, Fulci) and current high-theory reception
- Folk horror revival (*The Witch*, *Midsommar*, *Lamb*) and its relation to landscape writing
- Post-*Babadook* "A24 elevated horror" discourse — what it conceals
- Cronenberg as the philosopher he never claims to be
- Żuławski's *Possession* (1981) — most over-interpreted under-watched film of the era
- J-horror and K-horror moving from import-curio to canon
- Horror television (*Hill House*, *Channel Zero*) — still under-criticized

Scaffolding: Thacker, *In the Dust of This Planet*; Negarestani, *Cyclonopedia*; Carroll, *The Philosophy of Horror*; Linda Williams, "Film Bodies: Gender, Genre, and Excess" (*Film Quarterly*, 1991); Robin Wood, "An Introduction to the American Horror Film."

### YouTube (the more original opportunity)

The academic apparatus is thin — no one writes about YouTube the way Kael wrote about film. Veins:

- The video essay as critical form (Tony Zhou, Lindsay Ellis, Folding Ideas, Jacob Geller — Geller deserves a sustained study)
- Death of the long unstructured vlog; what was lost when "videos" became "content"
- ARGs and analog horror (*Marble Hornets*, *Petscop*, the *Backrooms*) as the first native digital folk-literature
- The channel-as-oeuvre problem
- MrBeast as a cultural object the discourse has been bad at reading
- Breadtube as a failed canon-formation attempt worth autopsying

Scaffolding (thinner, which is the opportunity): Hito Steyerl, *The Wretched of the Screen* (Sternberg, 2012) and "In Defense of the Poor Image" (*e-flux*, 2009); Lev Manovich; Kate Wagner on internet form.

### Lateral expansion

Video games as serious canon-work (Disco Elysium, Pathologic, Kentucky Route Zero, Outer Wilds). The slow novel (Krasznahorkai, Sebald, Lispector, Anne Carson, Ben Lerner). Post-streaming TV and which prestige shows survive (*The Leftovers*, *I May Destroy You* probably yes; *Game of Thrones* probably not). Micro-genre music canons built on Bandcamp/RYM that legacy critics haven't read. The built environment — Brutalism's rehabilitation, the under-criticized vernacular of American suburbia. Recent philosophical traditions moving cult-to-canon: CCRU, Fisher, Land, accelerationism, dark ecology.

The site does not need a beat. It needs a *practice*.

## What contribution actually means

A piece contributes when it does something the existing critical literature has not — a new juxtaposition, an unfamiliar source brought to bear on a familiar object, a sentence of close reading no one else has produced. The threshold is low but real. The `confidence` field enforces honesty: *log* and *unlikely* are permission to think out loud; *evergreen* / *highly-likely* is a claim on the apparatus. You will know the work is landing when, six months later, you read something else and think: my essay on X already named that.

## Using Claude — five permitted uses

1. **Reference retrieval** — librarian queries. Saves hours, costs nothing of you. Verify in primary texts before quoting.
2. **Adversarial reading** — paste the draft, ask: *What is the strongest objection? Where is the argument weakest? What would a Marxist / formalist / feminist horror scholar each find wrong with this?* Surfaces blind spots without producing prose.
3. **Register analysis** — *Where am I being vague? Which sentences do no work? Where does my register slip? Where am I performing rather than thinking?* Pointers, not replacements. Rewrite yourself.
4. **Constraint imposition** — *Force me to defend this in 200 words. Give me three counterexamples. What is the single weakest premise?* Produces friction you wouldn't otherwise generate.
5. **Corpus indexing** (relevant after ~30–40 pieces) — *Across these essays, what implicit claims about taste do I keep making? What objects do I keep returning to? Is there a thesis hiding in the pattern?* The use Claude is best at; no human reader can do this easily.

## Forbidden uses

- Writing the essay
- Polishing prose (erodes voice)
- "Expanding" paragraphs (produces filler)
- Generating opinions you don't have (if you don't have it, the essay isn't ready)
- Asking what to write about — the conversation is fine; the actual *next thing* must arrive from your own attention
- Imitating a critic's style (produces parody)
- Writing your Marginalia — the audit cannot be done by the audited party

## Weaning — four-stage schedule

- **Stage 1 (now → 6 months):** Drafting unaided. Revision admits Claude in adversarial, register-diagnostic, and constraint postures only. Every prompted move noted in Marginalia.
- **Stage 2 (6 → 12 months):** Drafting unaided. Revision admits Claude only as adversarial reader and reference librarian — no register analysis.
- **Stage 3 (12 → 18 months):** Essay form fully unaided, drafting and revision both. Claude permitted only for reference retrieval and corpus indexing.
- **Stage 4 (18 months on):** Claude permitted for site maintenance, research, and indexing only. No role in the production of prose.

The dates are arbitrary; the shape is not. The shape is the gradual evacuation of Claude from the parts of the process where voice and judgment are formed, and its retention only where it is a labor-saving tool.

## Two ancillary disciplines

**The no-Claude zone.** A designated hour, ideally morning, ideally longhand, model unreachable, work total. Cal Newport, *Deep Work* (2016) is the obvious reference; Annie Dillard's *The Writing Life* names the same hour in religious terms.

**The re-reading discipline.** Every six months, read the essay from a year before and edit it. If you can see weaknesses your then-self could not, you have grown. This is the only test of weaning that matters. The asymptote, done right, bends toward you — until you reach for Claude, find no question left to ask that you cannot ask yourself, and stop.
