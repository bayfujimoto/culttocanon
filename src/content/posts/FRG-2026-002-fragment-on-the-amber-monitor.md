The amber monitor was already obsolete when it was new. The green phosphor came first; the amber arrived as an ergonomic correction, easier on the eyes for the long shifts of the data-entry economy. By the time it shipped, the CRT itself was being supplanted by the LCD.

To use one now — to *prefer* one now — is not nostalgia. Nostalgia is the wish to restore. This is the more difficult posture: the acknowledgment that what the amber screen promised, the long quiet attention to text, was never realized at the scale its hardware presupposed, and that the form is now available again only because the future it implied did not arrive.
