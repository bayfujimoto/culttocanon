Genette's *Seuils* (1987) distinguishes peritext (front matter, footnotes, dust jacket) from epitext (interviews, letters, reviews). For a website, the peritext is what sits in the page chrome: the URL, the title bar, the navigation, the date. The epitext is what links to it from elsewhere — the other site that says *go read this*.

A digital garden's third pane, the per-piece companion, is *paratext that the author controls*. This is unusual. Most paratext is institutional — assigned by publishers, indexes, libraries. Here the writer assigns her own. The result is closer to the medieval gloss than to the modern apparatus: a paratext that argues with the text rather than merely framing it.

Worth more thought. Possibly an essay rather than a note.
