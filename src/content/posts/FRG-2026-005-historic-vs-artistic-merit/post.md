A work that changes the tides of history does not belong in the canon if it does not have sufficient artistic merit. Works with overflowing artistic value can be historically significant -- and most are -- but far fewer are those works that deliver the status quo and yet are identified as a turning point in a cultural narrative. 

*Top Gun: Maverick* (2022) is a popular and highly-rated movie in part due of its star power and adherence to a straightforward narrative and visual construction. Its technical achievements are impressive and the in-theater experience was thrilling, but it calls back to the 80s aesthetics of the original with all its eye-rolling quips and melodramatic crossfades and needle drops. It's structure is simple, consisting of a fall from grace, an unexpected task, a training montage, and redemption arc, along with many other time-worn story beats. Yet it stands tall because despite of its on-paper normativity, it does everything well -- and well enough to have brought back cautious crowds to the movie theater after the pandemic. Similarly, Avengers: Endgame does not tread unfamiliar ground -- it copiously borrows from the twenty-something Marvel films before it. It also marks an important turning point in the trajectory of contemporary film and the superhero fatigue that it accelerated. 

Does these movies deserve to be canonized? Or do they sit in the middle ground between cult and canon? (Is there a middle ground between cult and canon?) *Maverick* certainly does not qualify as cult; tens of millions of people saw it, and it was nominated for six Academy Awards, including a win in Best Sound and a Best Picture nod. Even more people saw Avengers: Endgame when it came out three years before, although the superhero genre has a massive cult following that propelled it to the mainstream.

This might be where one starts to create sub-canons. Maverick is in the canon of legacy sequels, and Endgame has its place in the superhero movie canon. 

*Note: This post focuses too much on canon and treats cult simply as a starting point towards canonization. And ==maybe it's wrong to ever ask if a movie should be canonized at all==, especially since there is no definitive canon for anything. Instead I should ask who is canonizing what and why? Once someone deems something as canonical, how does that change the perception of that text? Can canonical works have a cult following -- with fan theories and head canons? Or are they suddenly put into a sealed box, only observed from the outside?
